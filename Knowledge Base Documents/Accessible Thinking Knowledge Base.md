# Accessible Thinking Knowledge Base

## Source Scope

This knowledge base is based on the public pages of accessiblethinking.com, including About, Services, Research, Contact, Privacy Policy, and Branding.

## Organisation Profile

Accessible Thinking operates at the intersection of AI and accessibility. Its mission is to ensure that as AI reshapes society, disabled people are not left behind and are instead empowered by it. The organisation positions accessibility as the human dimension of AI: lived experience, disability perspective, and the people affected by automated systems. “Thinking” represents the technology dimension: how AI works, fails, and can be evaluated or improved.  

## Core Purpose

Accessible Thinking works towards a future where AI removes barriers rather than perpetuates them, and where disabled people’s participation in industry, arts, education, and public life is expanded by AI.  

## Values

Accessible Thinking’s stated values are inclusion by design, rigour, “nothing about us without us,” urgency, progress owned by the client, and constructive solution-building. These values position the organisation as evidence-led, disabled-perspective-led, practical, and focused on leaving client teams stronger rather than dependent.  

## Services

Accessible Thinking offers three main service areas: training, consulting, and research. These services focus on the intersection of AI and accessibility and are described as grounded in evidence and driven by disabled perspectives. Training is offered onsite and remotely for managers, business teams, and students. Consulting supports organisations from problem diagnosis through implementation. Research is used to keep advice evidence-based and to build the knowledge base for AI and accessibility.  

## Research Approach

Research is central to Accessible Thinking’s credibility. The organisation describes research as the mechanism that helps it question assumptions, test hypotheses, measure outcomes, and build evidence for its advice.  

## ASK: Accessible Survey Kit

ASK is described as a validated quantitative research platform that segments users by disability type and measures how different features perform across those segments. It is positioned as a way for organisations to generate disability-specific evidence that most cannot otherwise access.  

## Research in Practice

The site references a 2021 readability study in which 20 typefaces were tested and 337,350 user-preference data points were collected. The reported finding was that some commercially marketed dyslexia-friendly typefaces performed worse than several general sans-serif fonts.  

## Current Research Themes

Current project areas include tool development using quantitative data from real users, machine-learning tools that predict font accessibility based on disabled-user data, and training packages for organisations deploying AI without excluding disabled employees.  

## Team Profile

### Gareth Ford Williams

Gareth Ford Williams is Co-founder and CEO of Accessible Thinking. He founded the organisation to address a gap between organisations building AI systems and their ability to understand how those systems affect disabled users, alongside a gap in accessibility practitioners’ AI literacy. His background includes digital accessibility, public sector technology, and entertainment accessibility. He founded the BBC’s first Digital Accessibility Team, led accessibility for the launch of BBC iPlayer, and co-authored the Game Accessibility Guidelines.  

### Michael Mathews

Michael Mathews is Co-founder and CTO of Accessible Thinking. He co-founded the organisation after observing that accessibility work in education and employment was not connecting with the parallel AI conversation. His background includes digital development and education. He was an accredited teacher and worked for more than a decade at the BBC as Senior Software Engineer, Senior Research Engineer in Accessibility, and in BBC Academy. He oversaw creation of the Technical Accessibility Guidelines for the BBC UX&D Design System and delivers accessibility training, including apprenticeship teaching.  

## Brand Identity

The Accessible Thinking brand uses the abbreviation “AT,” echoing “Assistive Technology.” The elephant mark represents what the organisation describes as the “elephant in the room” in AI conversations: disabled people’s experiences.  

## Tone of Voice

The brand tone is authoritative but approachable, direct, evidence-grounded, human, and urgent without being alarmist. The writing style avoids corporate language and favours clear, practical wording.  

## Contact and Operating Model

Accessible Thinking is UK-based and works remotely with clients everywhere. The public contact email is contact@accessiblethinking.com. The site supports enquiries about training, consulting, research, partnerships, and other topics.  

## Privacy Position

The site states that it does not use cookies to collect, track, or share visitor data, and does not use analytics services, advertising networks, or third-party tracking technologies. Contact form data is used to respond to enquiries, and the privacy policy states that personal details are not shared with third parties for marketing purposes.
